The plant story

Tongkat Ali grows as a slender, slow-growing tree in the understory of Southeast Asian rainforests and can reach ten metres or more. Beneath the soil is the part that people have valued for centuries: the root.

In Malay, the name means "Ali's walking stick". In Indonesia the same plant is called Pasak Bumi, the "earth nail". Two names, one plant, and a tradition of knowledge passed from one generation to the next.

Other plants are sometimes sold under the same names, so look for the botanical name Eurycoma longifolia on the label. Ours is made from the root of that plant.

Traditional use

Traditionally used in Southeast Asian herbal practice to support general vitality, energy and well-being.

Questions people ask

Are Tongkat Ali, Pasak Bumi, Longjack and "Malaysian ginseng" the same thing?

They are names for the same plant, Eurycoma longifolia. Longjack is a trade name and "Malaysian ginseng" is a nickname. The plant is not related to true ginseng.

What is it traditionally used for?

Traditionally used in Southeast Asian herbal practice to support general vitality, energy and well-being. We make no claims about hormones, testosterone, libido or sexual function.

Does it raise testosterone?

We make no claims about hormones. Some studies have looked at this, but the published evidence is limited, and we have not reviewed enough of it to quote any. If this is your reason for considering it, please speak to a doctor.

How long until I notice anything?

Everyone is different, and we make no promises about results. Traditional herbs are part of a routine, so follow the directions consistently.

What are the side effects?

Restlessness, trouble sleeping and a raised body temperature have been reported, along with nausea, abdominal discomfort, diarrhoea or headaches. Rare cases of liver injury have been reported, mostly in bodybuilders, who may have been using other products as well. A United States National Institutes of Health database lists tongkat ali as a possible rare cause of liver injury. Long-term safety has not been adequately studied. Stop and speak to a healthcare professional if you feel unwell.

Has any regulator reviewed it?

In 2021 the European Food Safety Authority reviewed a standardised water extract of tongkat ali root. Based on laboratory and animal tests, it concluded that the extract has the potential to induce DNA damage, and that its safety had not been established under any condition of use. Our extract is also a water extract, so we think you should know. That opinion applies to the European Union, and it is one assessment. Please speak to a healthcare professional before using tongkat ali. The sources are listed at the end of this page.

Is it suitable for women?

Our product is not for use under 18, or by pregnant or nursing women. For anyone else, please speak to a healthcare professional. We do not give separate dosing for women.

Can I take it with my medication?

Speak to your doctor or pharmacist first. Some sources suggest it may affect how certain medicines are processed by the body.

When and how should I take it?

Take 1 capsule 1 to 2 hours before breakfast and before lunch, on an empty stomach, Monday to Friday, with weekends as rest days. Do not exceed 2 capsules in 24 hours. Early in the day is also sensible, since trouble sleeping is the most commonly reported side effect.

How long can I keep taking it?

Follow the Monday to Friday routine, with weekends off. Because long-term safety has not been adequately studied, please do not use it continuously for long periods without speaking to a healthcare professional.

How do I know it is real tongkat ali?

There are numerous cases of products falsely claiming to contain it. Check for the botanical name Eurycoma longifolia, and ask any brand for its Certificate of Analysis. Ours is made from the root of that plant, and our raw-material results are on this page.

What does it taste like?

The raw extract's certificate records a bitter, gradually astringent taste.

In a dry place below 30°C, away from direct sunlight. Check the label for the expiry date.
