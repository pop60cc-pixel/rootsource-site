01

The plant story

Sambiloto is the Indonesian name for Andrographis paniculata, an upright annual herb that grows to about a metre tall. It is native to India and Sri Lanka and is now found across tropical Asia.

Every part of the plant is intensely bitter, which is how it earned the name King of Bitters. In Ayurveda it is known as Kalmegh, meaning "dark cloud". In Malay it is Hempedu Bumi, "bile of the earth". In Indonesia it is Sambiloto, and it has long had a place in jamu, the traditional herbal medicine of Indonesia.

It has been used for a very long time in Ayurvedic and Siddha traditions as well. Look for the botanical name Andrographis paniculata on the label. Ours is made from the herb of that plant.

Traditional useTraditionally used in Indonesian herbal practice (jamu) to support general wellness and immune health.

Questions people ask

Honest answers, including the ones we would rather you heard from us. Search, or tap a topic.

Are Sambiloto, Andrographis, Kalmegh and green chiretta the same plant?

Yes. They are names for Andrographis paniculata. King of Bitters describes its taste, and "Indian echinacea" is a nickname, though it is a different plant from echinacea.

What is it traditionally used for?

Traditionally used in Indonesian herbal practice (jamu) to support general wellness and immune health. We make no claims about colds, flu, infections, blood sugar, blood pressure or the liver.

Will it help with colds, flu or infections?

We make no claims about colds, flu or infections. Some studies have looked at upper respiratory symptoms, but they vary in quality and we have not reviewed enough of them to quote any. If you are unwell, please see a healthcare professional.

How long until I notice anything?

Everyone is different, and we make no promises about results. Traditional herbs are part of a routine, so follow the directions consistently.

What are the side effects?

Reported side effects include a change in how things taste, rash, upset stomach, nausea, diarrhoea, headache and tiredness. Large doses may cause stomach discomfort, vomiting and loss of appetite, which appear to be linked to the bitter taste. Stop and speak to a healthcare professional if you feel unwell.

Can it cause an allergic reaction?

Yes. Andrographis can cause allergic reactions, and these can be serious. Do not use it if you are allergic to Andrographis or to plants of the same family (Acanthaceae). Stop using it and get medical help straight away if you have a rash, swelling or trouble breathing.

Is it suitable for women?

Our product is not for use under 18, or by pregnant or nursing women. Sources advise avoiding Andrographis during pregnancy and breastfeeding. For anyone else, please speak to a healthcare professional. We do not give separate dosing for women.

Can I take it with my medication?

Speak to your doctor or pharmacist first. Research on interactions is incomplete. Sources suggest it may affect blood pressure and blood sugar, and laboratory studies suggest it may change how some medicines are processed in the body. It is not for people on immunosuppressant medicines or with autoimmune conditions.

When and how should I take it?

Take 1 capsule with breakfast and 1 capsule with lunch, Monday to Friday, with weekends as rest days. Do not exceed the recommended dose, and drink plenty of water through the day.

How long can I keep taking it?

Sambiloto is not for indefinite use. We do not give a fixed number of weeks, so please speak to a healthcare professional about how long to take it.

What does it taste like?

Very bitter. The plant is regarded as one of the most bitter herbs in traditional medicine, and the raw extract's certificate records a bitter taste and a brownish-green colour.

What do 3:1 and 2.5% mean?

3:1 is an extraction ratio: roughly three parts of dried herb go into each part of finished extract. The certificate also records standardisation of 2.5%, meaning the extract is made to a set strength of a marker compound. It does not name that compound, and we are confirming it with our supplier.

How should I store it?

In a dry place below 30°C, away from direct sunlight. Check the label for the expiry date.

Check with your sport's anti-doping body before using any supplement.
